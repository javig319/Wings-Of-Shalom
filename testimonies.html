Mother and Daughter Together: Dancing a Path of Worship

Xintong

Hello, everyone. My name is Xintong. My daughter, Enoch, and I have been learning flag worship together with Wings of Shalom.

In the past, I knew very little about flag worship. Then, during a trip to Singapore, I happened to see a group of people worshiping with flags. The sight deeply moved me. I could truly sense the leading of the Holy Spirit, and a deep longing arose in my heart—I wanted so much to learn. At the time, my daughter Enoch and I even picked up flags ourselves and tried waving them in worship.

But after returning home, I could hardly find anywhere in China to learn flag worship, nor did I see churches practicing it. I later learned of a course in Hong Kong, but it lasted only a few days and cost more than 3,000 RMB, not including travel, meals, or lodging. So I had to let the idea go.

Yet while that longing remained in my heart, God prepared a way for me. He led me to discover Wings of Shalom. I enrolled together with my daughter and bought three pairs of flags—red, yellow, and white.

My body has always been quite stiff, and I had never learned dance in my life, so movement did not come naturally to me. But because of the love for God within my heart, I was deeply drawn to flag worship. To my amazement, even someone like me could learn these movements. It felt truly wonderful.

In our daily ministry, we serve children who struggle with depression, who have withdrawn from school, and some who turn to self-harm when they are overwhelmed. Every day, we face intense spiritual battles. All the staff at our school participate in morning worship, drawing strength from worship and from the Word of God so that we have what we need to serve these children throughout the day.

I am so grateful that God made a way for me. Of all seven days of the week, Monday morning is the only morning when we do not have corporate worship. Mondays are also the days when I am responsible for taking the children out—and the Wings of Shalom classes happened to be held on Mondays as well. So I adjusted our departure time to between 9:30 and 10:00 a.m., allowing Enoch and me to attend every class in full.

At nine years old, Enoch was probably the youngest student in the class. She never missed a single lesson and even received her graduation certificate. Physically, she is not naturally strong, and dancing has never been one of her strengths. Yet whenever she waves the flags in worship, her movements become so graceful and full of life. Every time I watch her, my heart is deeply touched.

Whenever my daughter and I worship with flags together, we experience the wonderful presence of God, and our hearts are filled with a spirit of joy. Enoch has also shared that the flag movements seem to come easily to her—she can often learn them right away. The sisters in our small group frequently pray for her, and she has noticed significant improvement in both her English and piano. Things that were once difficult for her to learn are gradually becoming easier.

One time, our school held an evening celebration. I am usually not someone who volunteers to perform, but I felt a prompting in my heart to offer a flag worship presentation, so I chose to follow that prompting.

While practicing in the auditorium, I realized that many of the brothers and sisters serving alongside us were just like I had once been—they had never seen flag worship before. Many came over to borrow our flags, and some of my coworkers began earnestly learning from us.

That evening, the principal arranged for our flag worship to be incorporated into every part of the program. The flags became a thread running throughout the entire celebration, and the gathering was especially blessed by God. Enoch and I ended up waving flags for nearly five hours. Amazingly, we were able to keep going, and at the time we did not even feel unbearably tired. To me, it was truly a miracle.

Afterward, two of my coworkers bought their own flags and signed up to learn flag worship as well. I am also especially grateful that after the course ended, we continued to have small-group prayer support. My group leader was Sister Liqing. Many times during prayer, tears would begin to flow because my heart was so deeply moved.

God is truly amazing. He brought together brothers and sisters in Christ from different parts of the world—people who otherwise might never have met—and gathered us together to praise His name, love one another, and bless one another. Even though we had never met face to face, there was a closeness among us that felt like family.

Today, I hang our three pairs of flags in my bedroom. I often sense His presence there, and each night I sleep with an extraordinary sense of peace. All glory and thanksgiving belong to God.

From “I Can't” to “God Can”

Grace Chen

This past March, Lucie invited members of our church to join Wings of Shalom. As I prayed about it, I felt the Lord stirring a desire in my heart to worship Him through dance, just as David danced before the Lord. I had never learned to dance, but I believed that worshiping with flags would help lift my spirit and allow me to express my love and praise to God in a deeper way.

When I shared this desire with Sister Lily, she was so excited that she immediately connected me with the ministry and helped me register. On the very last day of registration, I became a member of Wings of Shalom.

The first few classes went well, and I was able to follow along. However, when we began learning the “Pillar of Cloud and Pillar of Fire” movements, I quickly realized how limited my hands and wrists were. The figure-eight motions required coordination and flexibility that I simply didn't have. Even with Instructor Lucie's patient instruction and constant encouragement, I just couldn't get it right.

After class, I kept practicing on my own, hoping I would eventually improve. But by Monday, I still hadn't made a breakthrough. At over seventy years old, I began to think that perhaps I had reached the limits of what my mind and body could learn. I honestly felt it was time to give up.

The next morning, I joined Wings of Shalom's “365 Days of Declaring God's Word” Morning Altar at 6:30 a.m. As I declared God's Word and prayed, I simply told the Lord, “I've done all I can. The rest is in Your hands.”

Later that morning, as we worshiped with flags under Clara's leadership, something incredible happened. The moment I began to worship, my flags moved effortlessly. To my amazement, I was performing the “Pillar of Cloud and Pillar of Fire” routine. The figure-eight movements flowed naturally. My left and right hands moved together in perfect rhythm, and my wrists traced smooth figure eights without getting tangled.

I was overwhelmed with joy, gratitude, and awe. In that moment, I experienced Jehovah Jireh—our God who provides. He supplied exactly what I lacked at exactly the right time, and He enabled me to complete the entire semester. All glory, honor, and praise belong to our loving God. Amen!

Testimony

A Journey into Deeper Worship

Lily Tsang

Every time I worship with the flags, I am deeply moved by God's love. Looking back, I realize that joining Wings of Shalom last September was truly a miracle. Before then, I knew very little about flag worship and had never imagined using flags to worship the Lord. Since this desire could only have come from the prompting of the Holy Spirit, every time I lift the flags in my hands, my heart is filled with awe, gratitude, and reverence for God.

After completing two semesters of training—beginning with the Beginner Class last fall and continuing with the Advanced Class this spring—I thank God for leading me into a deeper understanding of flag worship. The more I learn, the more I experience His presence and His love. I now understand more personally the truth of His Word: “Draw near to God, and He will draw near to you.” (James 4:8)

One of the greatest blessings this semester has been learning more expressive and dynamic flag worship techniques, along with the importance of breathing in a way that engages the body, soul, and spirit in worship. Even more precious has been the weekly nourishment through God's Word.

God has also given all of us an incredible gift—a leader who truly fears and loves the Lord. Through Clara's example, I have learned what it means to serve God with all my heart, all my strength, and all my mind. This experience reminds me of Jesus' words in John 15:16: “You did not choose Me, but I chose you and appointed you so that you might go and bear fruit—fruit that will last.” I thank God for choosing me, and I will continue to run the race faithfully alongside Wings of Shalom, lifting high the Banner of the Lord's Victory wherever He leads.

Testimony

Learning to Worship with Freedom

Xiaojuan

When I was young, I always loved dancing. Having the opportunity now to combine my love for dance with worshiping God through flag worship is truly a privilege and an honor.

I am deeply grateful to our leader, Clara, for teaching us the biblical truths and spiritual foundations of flag worship. Thank you to Instructor Lucie for your gentle, patient guidance and for carefully helping every sister learn each movement. Thank you also to group leader Surpris for faithfully leading the prayer time after each class. And to all the other instructors whose names I have not mentioned, thank you for your quiet, behind-the-scenes dedication and service.

I have come to understand that worship is centered on God. It is about offering our whole being—body, soul, and spirit—to become His vessel, experiencing the presence and leading of the Holy Spirit. The focus is not on how well I perform, but on whether I am responding to God with a heart that expresses my love, reverence, and adoration for Him.

Although I still sometimes worry about whether I can perform certain flag movements correctly or whether my dancing looks graceful enough, my prayer is that my Heavenly Father will help me take my eyes off myself and fix them on His presence and His love, so that I can fully relax and worship and praise Him with freedom and joy.
